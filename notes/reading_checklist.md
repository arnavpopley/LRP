# Paper reading checklist (LRP 241)

Answer every question in one line, in your own words. If you can't answer one without reopening the paper, that's the part to reread. Nothing else.

Time box: 90 min for a READ paper, 20 min for a section of one.

---

## A. Before you start (2 min)

1. Which objective does this paper feed: 1 (chemistry), 2 (method), 3 (estimates), or 4 (leakage)?
2. What is the one question I need this paper to answer? (Write it at the top of your notes.)
3. What does the extraction file already say about it? (Skim its entry, including any conflicts listed at the top.)

## B. The claim (abstract + conclusions, 10 min)

4. What is the paper's main claim, in one sentence?
5. What is the headline number, with its uncertainty?
6. What does that uncertainty actually cover, and what does it leave out?

## C. The method (tables + methods, 30 min)

**If it estimates GWP or a budget:**

7. Model: box, 2-D, 3-D, or ensemble? Which one?
8. Experiment: pulse or steady state? Emissions-driven or fixed surface concentration? How big is the perturbation?
9. Which effects are included: CH₄, tropospheric O₃, stratospheric H₂O, stratospheric O₃, aerosols?
10. Key assumptions, with values:
    - soil sink (Tg/yr)
    - H₂ lifetime (yr)
    - CH₄ feedback factor or perturbation lifetime
    - how OH is treated
11. Where are emissions placed (global pattern, a hemisphere, or a single site)?

**If it is about leakage or detection:**

7. Measured, modelled, or assumed? If modelled, what are the rates scaled from (natural-gas data, lab tests, expert judgement)?
8. A percentage of what: production, throughput, or consumption?
9. Which stages are in, and which are out? (End use? Venting versus leaks? Permeation?)
10. What detection limit or sensitivity is stated, in ppm or ppb?

## D. Critical thinking (15 min)

12. What limitations do the authors admit?
13. What do they *not* say? Check for: a single model, linearity assumed, shared model biases (Yang's Table 1), a fixed soil sink, or a missing stratosphere.
14. Does it agree or disagree with the papers I've already read? Which assumption explains the difference?
15. Is the evidence observational or model-only?
16. Would my supervisor push back on anything here? On what?

## E. Figures (5 min)

17. Which figure carries the main result?
18. What does it show, and what does it hide?
19. Would I put it in the report? If yes, what would my caption say it proves?

## F. Write-up (close the PDF, 20 min)

20. Two sentences: what this paper adds to *my* argument.
21. Numbers for the tracker, each with a page number.
22. One sharp question for my supervisor or the authors.
23. Reopen the PDF only to add the page numbers.

---

## Notes template (Meeting 1 style)

```
[Author Year], [Journal]:
• Objective: [1/2/3/4]; question: [...]

Claim:
• [one sentence] —> [headline number ± range] (p.X)

Method:
   A. [model; experiment type]
   B. [effects included]
   C. [soil sink; lifetime; feedback factor] (p.X)

Critique:
• Admitted: [...] (p.X)
• Unstated: [...]
• vs other papers: [agrees/disagrees with X because Y]

Figure:
• Fig N (p.X): shows [...]; hides [...]; use? [y/n]

For my report:
• [2 sentences, own words]
• Question: [...]
```
